Polenta

Tags: Vegan, Gluten-Free, Dairy-Free, Nut-Free, Soy-Free

Source: Insight Retreat Center

Scale recipe: or ×

Note: this version is simplified to be all vegan, no cheese — serve cheese on the side for the whole meal.

Yield: 2–3 full pans depending on supper plans (only 1 pan needed for a group of ~36 with no supper; ask how many pans are needed — usually 2 if no supper or a supper "minestrone" soup is planned, 3 if a supper casserole is planned). Ingredient quantities below are per full pan.

Equipment

  • If doing supper: 3 full pans — serve 2 and save the third for supper. If no supper, just 2 full-size pans.
  • Use 4" (medium depth) pans so they don't slosh when going into the oven.
  • If making 3 pans: since there are only 2 4"-deep full pans, use 2 4" half pans or a 6"-deep one for the third, and save that third pan for the supper leftover.

Ingredients (per full pan)

Preparation

  1. Mix and bake polenta: Preheat oven to 425°.
  2. Spray pans with spray oil.
  3. Mix polenta and seasonings in each of the pans you are using.
  4. Leave pans uncovered and put them in the oven about 10:15. Set a timer for an hour.
  5. Stir & maybe cover: About 11:15, carefully (with hot pad gloves, and maybe with help) remove the pans and stir.
  6. Peek every 20 minutes or so. If the top is getting too brown, put the lid on. If done, turn off the ovens and leave the pans inside to stay warm.
  7. Maybe stir in some Earth Balance at the end.
  8. Alternate (stovetop, from Lael): Some cooks prefer this stovetop version — it frees up the oven so you can pre-roast more of the ratatouille veggies (which improves that dish), but it takes more attention. Bring 10 quarts water to a boil (get this going early, ~10:45); be sure a heat deflector is under the pot.
  9. Add ⅓ cup salt.
  10. About 11:20, slowly pour 10 cups polenta into the pot, whisking constantly to avoid lumps. Keep whisking while it thickens.
  11. When thickened, turn the flame down to a bare simmer.
  12. Stir every 10 minutes for 40 minutes — this long simmer brings out the sweet corn flavor.
  13. Add more boiling (tea machine) water if too stiff.
  14. Stir in some Earth Balance (or other vegan butter) to taste.
  15. Note on using leftovers for the next night's supper: if there's quite a bit left (~4 qts), it's best to do this right after lunch, during the put-away period: grease a sheet pan with olive oil, spread the polenta on it to ~½–¾" thick (¾ of a sheet pan is enough), sprinkle with basil and vegan parmesan, and put the sheet in the fridge (maybe covered with foil or another sheet pan).
  16. At 5:00 on supper day, put in a 400° oven for a half hour. Cut into ~7x8 pieces. (Oven-baked polenta could probably be used the same way, but it's harder to spread.)
