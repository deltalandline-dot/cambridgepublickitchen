Equipment  if supper: 3 full pans.  Serve the 2 and save the other for supper.  If no supper, just 2 full size.  Use the 4” (medium depth) so it doesn’t slosh when putting in the oven.

Ingredients:  Note: simplified to be all Vegan no-cheese, serve cheese on the side for the whole meal.

NOTE: If making 3 pans:  We only have 2 4” deep full pans, so use 2 4” half pans or a 6” deep one for the third and save the 3rd for supper leftover.

Instructions

Mix and bake Polenta

Preheat oven to 425.  

Spray pans with spray oil.

Mix polenta and seasonings in each of the pans you are using.

LEAVE PANS UNCOVERED and put them in the oven about 10:15.  Set a timer for an hour.

Stir & Maybe Cover

About 11:15, carefully with hot pad gloves (and maybe with help) remove the pans and stir.

Peek every 20 minutes or so.  If top is getting too brown, put the lid on.  If done, turn off ovens and leave pans inside to stay warm.              

Maybe stir in some earth balance at the end.

Alternate versions preferred by some cooks, notes more than recipe

STOVETOP:  something like this:  use 4:1 water to polenta as above.  So the equivalent of the standard 2 oven pans would be maybe 40 c (= 10qt) of water to 10 cups polenta.  Bring 20c=5qts water to boil.  Mix polenta with 10 c cold water to make slurry.  Add to boiling water.  Stir very often, slowing adding the final 10 cups of water as it thickens.  Use  your judgement if more water is needed to make a thick but ladle-able consistency.   Salt to taste.

Notes from cook Susan Black on another version that makes little squares  in a sheetpan:

It’s prepared on the stove top, using the amount that the recipe calls for (ie same as stovetop above)

It’ss boullion, same amount as other recipe, earth balance, and whatever herb one likes, just not as much as the other recipe, 1/4 to half. And no pepper.. 

Key is to stir in the earth balance as it is becoming polenta, not in the water. The closer to done u add the earth balance u need less and it will taste creamy. Quantity of earth balance is about 1/2 cup then to taste. Olive oil gives it that little herbaceous taste, u can mix in both. 

Pour into a sheet pan when done, smooth and creamy,  where it cools (fast) and can be cut into triangles or squares, whatever. I cook it in two separate pots, 1 per sheet pan. That’s why u can use half, because one sheet pan can be cut into 50 slices that are medium to large. Make sure to grease the sheet pan before pouring. 

But if u make two sheet pans the other one can be used the next day, or frozen and used days later. Freezes well. If used the next day it could be fried lightly in earth balance, or oil, crispy then. Or, simply brush olive oil or melted earth blance over the top and bake, low as u are reheating,  it will crisp up a bit. 

The creaminess comes from the earth balance, but one could also use olive oil, or any vegan fat. 

Even fancier idea (maybe to make for a supper leftover portion):   say you roasted some mushrooms, u could pour the polenta on the mushrooms on the sheet pan and cool, and now u have toasted mushroom polenta. Or any veg for that matter, zucchini, bell pepper, really as long as it is roasted and spread out evenly u can just pour the polenta over it and yum.
