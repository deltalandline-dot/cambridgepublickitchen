Alt Notes on Recipe Polenta

Tags: Vegan, Gluten-Free, Dairy-Free, Nut-Free, Soy-Free

Source: Insight Retreat Center

Scale recipe: or ×

Simplified to be all vegan, no cheese — serve cheese on the side for the whole meal. If making 3 pans: we only have 2 4” deep full pans, so use 2 4” half pans or a 6” deep one for the third and save that one for a supper leftover.

Yield: ask KM how many pans — usually 2 if no supper (or a supper "minestrone" soup is planned), 3 if a supper casserole is planned (only 1 for a group of ~36 with no supper use). Quantities below are per full pan.

Equipment

  • If supper: 3 full pans (serve 2, save the other for supper); if no supper, just 2 full-size pans
  • Use the 4” (medium depth) pans so it doesn't slosh when putting in the oven

Ingredients (per full pan)

Preparation

  1. Mix and bake polenta: Preheat oven to 425.
  2. Spray pans with spray oil.
  3. Mix polenta and seasonings in each of the pans you are using.
  4. Leave pans uncovered and put them in the oven about 10:15. Set a timer for an hour.
  5. Stir & maybe cover: About 11:15, carefully with hot pad gloves (and maybe with help) remove the pans and stir. Peek every 20 minutes or so — if the top is getting too brown, put the lid on. If done, turn off ovens and leave pans inside to stay warm. Maybe stir in some Earth Balance at the end.
  6. Stovetop alternate: Use 4:1 water to polenta as above, so the equivalent of the standard 2 oven pans would be maybe 40 c (=10 qts) of water to 10 cups polenta. Bring 20 c = 5 qts water to a boil. Mix polenta with 10 c cold water to make a slurry. Add to the boiling water. Stir very often, slowly adding the final 10 cups of water as it thickens. Use your judgement if more water is needed for a thick but ladle-able consistency. Salt to taste.
  7. Sheet-pan alternate (notes from cook Susan Black, makes little squares): Prepared on the stovetop, using the same amount as the stovetop version above — same amount of bouillon, plus Earth Balance and whatever herb you like (just not as much as the other recipe, ¼ to half), and no pepper. Key is to stir in the Earth Balance as it is becoming polenta, not into the water — the closer to done you add it, the less you need and it will taste creamier. Quantity of Earth Balance is about ½ cup, then to taste. Olive oil gives it a bit of herbaceous taste; you can mix in both. Pour into a sheet pan when done, smooth and creamy — it cools quickly and can be cut into triangles or squares. Cooking it in two separate pots (1 per sheet pan) means each pan can use half quantities, since one sheet pan can be cut into about 50 medium-to-large slices; grease the sheet pan before pouring. If you make two sheet pans, the extra one can be used the next day or frozen for later (it freezes well); if used the next day it can be fried lightly in Earth Balance or oil for a crispy texture, or simply brushed with olive oil or melted Earth Balance on top and baked low (while reheating) to crisp up a bit. The creaminess comes from the Earth Balance, but olive oil or any vegan fat could be used instead. Fancier idea (maybe for a supper leftover portion): pour the polenta over roasted mushrooms (or any roasted vegetable — zucchini, bell pepper, etc.) spread on the sheet pan and let cool, for toasted mushroom (or vegetable) polenta.
